1. What KashLink is

KashLink is a piece of software. It turns an amount of USDC on Arc — Circle's blockchain, on which USDC is also the currency used to pay network fees — into a link that anyone holding it can claim.

KashLink is non-custodial. Every link is a freshly generated key pair created in your browser. The private key is placed in the link itself; its address identifies the link in the KashLink escrow contract, a smart contract deployed on Arc whose source code is published and verified on the Arc block explorer. When you create a link, your wallet deposits the USDC into that contract. When the link is claimed, the contract pays the USDC to the address the link holder names. We do not receive, store, hold or have access to the key, to the funds in the contract, or to your wallet.

The contract has an owner (us) whose only powers are to change the fee rate (capped at 5%), the fee floor and the address fees are paid to. The owner cannot withdraw escrowed funds, block or reverse a claim or a refund, pause the contract or change its code. There is no upgrade mechanism.

KashLink does not hold balances, offer accounts, convert currencies or move funds on anyone's behalf. Every transaction is signed by a key that you or the link holder controls and is broadcast directly to the Arc network. Nothing in the Service is financial, investment, legal or tax advice.

KashLink is an independent project. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by or operated by Circle, Arc Network Services LLC or any wallet provider.

3. How a link works, and what that means for you

Whoever holds the link controls the money. A KashLink contains the private key that authorises the claim. Anyone who obtains the link — by being sent it, by seeing it on a screen, by finding it in a chat backup, a clipboard, a browser history or a screenshot — can claim the funds to an address of their choosing, and you will have no recourse against us. Treat a KashLink exactly as you would treat cash.

In particular, you acknowledge and accept that:

  • There is no recovery of a claimed link. If a link is intercepted or claimed by someone other than the intended recipient, the funds cannot be recovered by us or by anyone else.
  • There is no identity check. The Service does not verify who opens or claims a link. A claim is valid if it is sent from the link's address, regardless of who is holding the link.
  • Losing the link means losing the ability to share it, not the money. The Service stores the keys of links you create in your browser's local storage so you can re-share them. If you clear site data or lose the device, you can no longer share those links, but you can still take back any unclaimed link after it expires using the wallet that created it.
  • Sharing channels are not ours. Links are usually shared through messaging apps. Their security, backups and retention are outside our control.
  • A recipient may bypass the app. Because the link is a private key, a recipient may import it into any wallet and call the contract's claim function directly.
  • Only trust the genuine app. Anyone can copy open-source software and host a lookalike that steals keys. We are not responsible for sites, apps or links that imitate KashLink. Check the address you are visiting, and only open links from people you know.

4. Fees

ItemAmount
Service fee1% of the link amount, with a minimum of $0.10.
Prepaid claim gas$0.01, sent to the link's own address so the recipient can claim without holding anything first.

Both:

  • are paid by the sender on top of the amount, so the recipient receives the full round amount shown when the link was created;
  • are charged when the link is created and are not returned, whether the link is later claimed or taken back. A link therefore costs the same either way, which is what makes creating and cancelling links pointless as an attack;
  • are shown on the review screen before you send;
  • are enforced by the escrow contract, and the service fee is retained by us and is non-refundable.

Whatever part of the prepaid gas the claim does not use stays at the link's address and is not recovered. The fee schedule in force is whatever the contract reports at the time you create a link; we may change it, within the contract's 5% cap, for links created after the change. Network fees you pay from your own wallet when creating or refunding a link are set by the Arc network, not by us.

5. The escrow contract

The escrow contract is the part of the Service that holds funds. You acknowledge that:

  • the contract is immutable. Its address and verified source code are published in the app's repository and on the Arc block explorer, and you can read exactly what it does before you use it;
  • a claim is paid to whatever address the link holder names. The contract cannot tell a right address from a wrong one. USDC sent to an address you do not control is lost;
  • the contract will refuse to pay an address that the Arc network's blocklist prohibits, and a claim or refund to such an address will fail;
  • smart contracts can contain bugs. The contract has automated tests but has not been independently audited. Do not put more into a link than you are prepared to lose.

6. Returning an unclaimed link

When you create a link you choose how long it stays claimable — by default 7 days. After that time, if nobody has claimed it, the wallet that created it may call the contract's refund function to take the full link amount back. The Service offers a button for this; the contract enforces it. It is not automatic: the refund happens only when you ask for it and sign it with your wallet.

A link remains claimable until it is refunded, including after its expiry time. Whichever of claim and refund reaches the network first wins; the other fails.
